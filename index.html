Why I built this

When I was a kid I used to take the blank maps of Canada my teachers handed out, bring home a stack of photocopies, and spend hours filling in every city and town and lake I could think of. I just loved maps. Later, in Cégep, I took a world geography course where the entire grade was one exam - name every country on a map.

It still pays off in small ways. You meet someone, you know where they're from. You hear something in the news, you can actually picture it. It's one of those things that sounds like useless trivia until it isn't.

My son started learning world geography a couple years ago. I looked at the apps out there and honestly, I thought I could do better. So here we are.

What it's actually like

Knowing where a place is turns out to matter more than being able to rattle off a list, so most modes point at one country and ask you to name it. Name All is the exception, and it's the trophy.

  • Spelling is forgiven within reason. "Liechtenstien" and "Madagasgar" both count. Drop a letter entirely and it won't save you.
  • Old and common names work - Burma, Holland, Swaziland, Czechia, England.
  • The globe flies to each country as you name it, so you see where it actually sits.
  • The daily recap splits your 10 into the ones you knew cold, the ones that needed a hint, and the ones you missed.
  • Installs to your home screen like an app and works with no signal.
  • 200 places, not 195 - the UN's list plus Taiwan, Kosovo, Western Sahara, Somaliland and Northern Cyprus. Those are the ones people can never place, which is exactly why they're in. Here's the full reasoning.
